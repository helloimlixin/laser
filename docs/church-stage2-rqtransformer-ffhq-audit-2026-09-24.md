The spatial/depth architecture follows released RQ-Transformer: the spatial transformer receives completed earlier sites, and the depth transformer receives shifted cumulative depth context. LASER changes each depth event into a pair:

`p(a_t, c_t | h_t) = p(a_t | h_t) p(c_t | h_t, a_t)`

Here `h_t` includes every completed earlier atom–coefficient pair. The pair embedding includes the frozen atom vector, the learned coefficient-token embedding, and the physical contribution `c_t * dictionary[a_t]`. The coefficient micro-transformer consumes the predicted history state and a projection of the current dictionary atom vector. Generation writes the sampled coefficient into the history before predicting the next atom. Both the restored Church implementation and saved FFHQ trainer implement this factorization.

Final OMP coefficients are fitted jointly with a site's support. That does not itself invalidate this chain-rule factorization: a previous coefficient is an earlier modeled random variable. What would invalidate it is exposing the current coefficient or a future atom to an earlier predictor. The numerical perturbation tests found neither. Comments and tests about the older atom-only depth path should not be applied to the enabled full-pair path.

With identical tiny-model weights, the vendored RQ backbone agrees with released RQ-Transformer to maximum output error `4.77e-7` and gradient error `2.98e-8` in FP32. The Church audit perturbed both components at every event on a 2×2×4 grid; all 64 predictor checks passed. An incremental sampler containing only completed history matched parallel teacher forcing within `4.77e-7`. The analogous FFHQ 2×2×2 checks passed within `3.58e-7`, using the recovered backbone. Dictionary-vector and history gradients were nonzero and finite. The Church checks also passed with the deployed short-sequence attention optimization. These are implementation checks, not a guarantee of equivalent floating-point trajectories across hardware.

RQ's stochastic code sampling and soft-label objective concern residual codebook vectors. LASER instead uses sparse atom selections plus scalar coefficient distributions, a coefficient-conditioned history, and atom/coefficient loss weights of 1.5:1. The numerical audit independently verified that the active loss and its gradients equal `(1.5 * atom_NLL + coefficient_CE) / 2.5`, including accumulation scaling. These are deliberate modeling changes, so this remains a LASER adaptation rather than an exact reproduction of RQ's training task. The public release supplies the model and sampling pipeline but not its stage-2 training pipeline; an exact reproduction claim would exceed the available provenance. [Released code](https://github.com/kakaobrain/rq-vae-transformer), [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.01941).

The newly identified masking mismatch wastes some training probability on atoms that generation forbids. Restoring the earlier Church behavior aligns that constraint. The bank has useful but limited diversity. On 100,000 sampled spatial sites, its 16 entries contain **4.80 distinct ordered supports on average**: median 4, 90th percentile 10, and 14.56% of sites have just one distinct support. A complete support/coefficient trajectory is selected together independently per site; atoms and coefficients are not mixed between trajectories. This is more stochastic than one fixed support, but it is not 16 independent distinct choices everywhere or unlimited online resampling.

At physical coefficient target temperature 0.125, measured target standard deviations are approximately `[0.250, 0.250, 0.248, 0.244]` across depths. Because the Lloyd–Max centers are nonuniform and target probabilities are proportional to `exp(-(c-center)^2 / temperature)`, this is a categorical smoothing kernel, not an exactly integrated Gaussian. Measured RMS mean shifts are `[0.0080, 0.0083, 0.0141, 0.0301]`. FFHQ's temperature 0.5 operates in normalized units with scales `[36.2083, 8.5833]`; comparing the raw temperature numbers is misleading. This audit does not establish that increasing coefficient noise would help. Earlier short continuation pilots with fresh OMP sampling or cell-integrated targets did not beat their retained baseline, and were not fresh-training controls.

FFHQ's geometry objective is also an approximation: it multiplies an expected candidate atom vector by a coefficient expectation conditioned on the teacher atom. That generally differs from the full joint expectation `sum_a p(a|h) * dictionary[a] * E[c|h,a]`. This does not break the main autoregressive model, but it limits the objective's claimed interpretation. The new Church trial keeps geometry off, as in the successful Church control. A future geometry experiment should evaluate coefficient distributions for each candidate atom and be tested separately.
