\paragraph{Sparse coefficient tokenization.}
Sparse representations combine discrete atom identities with real-valued coefficients. To model them with a categorical autoregressive prior, we construct a finite vocabulary in which each token identifies an atom and one of its admissible coefficient values. A latent feature is then represented by a short sequence of additive dictionary contributions.

Let $D_{\mathrm{tok}}=[d_1,\ldots,d_M]\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times M}$ be a tokenization dictionary with unit-norm columns. For each atom $d_j$, we associate $L_j$ distinct, nonzero coefficient levels $\{a_{j,b}\}_{b=1}^{L_j}$, which may be signed. Assign each pair $(j,b)$ an integer index
\begin{equation}
    \iota(j,b)=1+\sum_{m<j}L_m+(b-1),
    \qquad
    \mathcal{V}=\left\{0,\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{M}L_j\right\}.
\end{equation}
The token-to-vector mapping is
\begin{equation}
    e(0)=\boldsymbol{0},
    \qquad
    e\bigl(\iota(j,b)\bigr)=a_{j,b}d_j.
    \label{eq:token-contribution}
\end{equation}
The vocabulary contains $1+\sum_j L_j$ entries, including a zero contribution, and is shared across all residual steps. Thus each integer has a fixed vector contribution throughout the sequence. Allowing $L_j$ to vary allocates coefficient resolution across atoms under a finite vocabulary budget.

We encode an encoder feature $\boldsymbol{z}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ by greedy residual assignment. Starting from $\boldsymbol{r}_{i,0}=\boldsymbol{z}_i$, we compute
\begin{equation}
    t_{i,k}
    =\underset{v\in\mathcal{V}}{\operatorname{arg\,min}}
      \left\|\boldsymbol{r}_{i,k-1}-e(v)\right\|_2^2,
    \qquad
    \boldsymbol{r}_{i,k}
    =\boldsymbol{r}_{i,k-1}-e(t_{i,k}),
    \label{eq:token-assignment}
\end{equation}
for $k=1,\ldots,K$. Each step jointly selects an atom and coefficient level to approximate the current residual. The sequence follows residual selection order, and repeated selections of an atom are permitted. The reconstructed latent feature is
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\boldsymbol{z}}_i
    =\sum_{k=1}^{K}e(t_{i,k}).
    \label{eq:token-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
Once selected, a contribution remains fixed during subsequent steps. The zero token allows fewer than $K$ effective contributions, and decoding requires only fixed lookups and addition.

This construction preserves an explicit sparse dictionary representation. Define the aggregated coefficient of atom $j$ by
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\gamma}_{i,j}
    =\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{b=1}^{L_j}
      a_{j,b}\,
      \mathbf{1}\!\left[t_{i,k}=\iota(j,b)\right].
\end{equation}
Then $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{z}}_i=D_{\mathrm{tok}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_i$ and $\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_i\|_0\leq K$. The discrete reconstructions therefore form a finite subset of the dictionary's union of subspaces. Coefficient resolution controls the available magnitudes along each atom, while residual depth controls the number of additive contributions and the token sequence length.

The tokenization dictionary and coefficient levels are fitted to encoder features with the encoder and decoder fixed, and are subsequently frozen during prior training.

\paragraph{Stochastic residual teacher.}
For autoregressive prior training, we use a stochastic residual teacher following RQ-Transformer~\citep{lee2022rqvae}, adapted to the shared atom--coefficient vocabulary. Starting from $\boldsymbol{r}^{\mathrm{tr}}_{i,0}=\boldsymbol{z}_i$, the conditional distribution at depth $k$ is
\begin{equation}
    q_{i,k}(v)=\frac{\exp\!\left(-\|\boldsymbol{r}^{\mathrm{tr}}_{i,k-1}-e(v)\|_2^2/\tau_k\right)}{\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}\exp\!\left(-\|\boldsymbol{r}^{\mathrm{tr}}_{i,k-1}-e(u)\|_2^2/\tau_k\right)},
    \qquad \tau_k>0.
    \label{eq:token-soft-target}
\end{equation}
We sample $t^{\mathrm{tr}}_{i,k}\sim q_{i,k}$ and update $\boldsymbol{r}^{\mathrm{tr}}_{i,k}=\boldsymbol{r}^{\mathrm{tr}}_{i,k-1}-e(t^{\mathrm{tr}}_{i,k})$. Each subsequent teacher distribution is therefore conditioned on the sampled prefix. The prior is trained by soft-target cross-entropy over the full vocabulary, using the sampled tokens as causal context and the corresponding distributions $q_{i,k}$ as prediction targets. This exposes the prior to alternative discrete decompositions while assigning greater probability to contributions that better approximate the current residual.

At generation time, the prior predicts joint atom--coefficient IDs autoregressively, and the decoder receives their sum in Eq.~\eqref{eq:token-reconstruction}. The encoder, decoder, tokenization dictionary, and coefficient levels remain frozen throughout prior training.
