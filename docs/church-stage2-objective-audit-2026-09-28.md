The audit found concrete limitations in the current compound-code representation and objective, but no demonstrated causal-mask, pair-conditioning, gradient-reduction, or checkpoint-initialization bug. These limitations are plausible contributors to poor generalization; this audit does not establish which causes the FID plateau. The running experiment was not changed or interrupted. All new probes ran on CPU.

The audited run is [church-ffhq-joint-scratch-20260928](https://wandb.ai/helloimlixin-rutgers/laser/runs/church-ffhq-joint-scratch-20260928). Its live, frozen sources are under `/tmp/laser-church-ffhq-joint-scratch-20260928`. Evidence and executable probes are preserved under `outputs/church-stage2-objective-audit-20260928/`.

1. Whole-vector scoring is restricted by an anchor-centered proposal.

   `full_vocab_teacher.py:143–167` constructs a residual for each pair with all other original cached pairs held fixed. Each depth gets the cached atom and only one proposed alternate atom, with three coefficient candidates for each. It then enumerates and scores the 6^4 complete combinations. Sampling and labels are internally consistent within that pool, including cross terms, but good coordinated changes can be absent before full-vector scoring begins. Positive full-vocabulary proposal probability is not the same as an unrestricted full-vocabulary Gibbs target, and proposal probabilities are not corrected to produce such a target.

   A small discrete CPU example has two exact decompositions of `[1,1]`: the canonical basis with coefficients `[1,1]`, and atoms `[.8,.6]`, `[.6,-.8]` with coefficients `[1.4,-.2]`. No coefficient is fitted. The exact uniform-sequence Gibbs distribution gives the latter ordered tuple probability 0.1656 at temperature .005. The current candidate builder's probability of even including it is at most 2.04e-8, because both needed coefficients are far from their individual anchor-conditioned proposal modes. None of 2,048 sampled pools included it. This establishes a failure mode, not its frequency on Church or the behavior at the production temperature.

   A second synthetic example gives 64 identical dictionary atoms identical vector meaning. The current teacher assigns the designated anchor about 53.5% probability, versus 1.56% under a uniform-atom full Gibbs law. The current teacher is a valid anchor-dependent augmentation distribution, but it does not depend only on reconstructed-vector geometry.

   A real Church CPU probe enumerated all 24 permutations of the four original atom/physical-coefficient pairs on 32 training and 32 held-out images (4,096 sites). Coefficients were rounded to the unchanged destination-depth grids; out-of-range permutations were rejected, never clipped or fitted. Every site had at least one valid nonidentity alternative. There were 14.93 alternatives per training site and 15.13 per held-out site on average. Every valid alternative was below the registered .242614 distortion ceiling; the largest full-vector error was only .000221. Thus near-equivalent complete representations actually exist in these Church codes, at rounding-level error. These permutations reuse the same unordered support set. They do not establish abundant alternatives with different support sets, quantify production proposal inclusion, or justify randomizing away useful coarse-to-fine ordering.

2. The likelihood still distinguishes different encodings of the same vector.

   The model factorization `p(a,c|h)=p(a|h)p(c|a,h)` is valid. The two heads need not be replaced by an enormous flat compound classifier. However, `cartesian_combination_cross_entropy` supervises ordered token identities. Full-vector distances determine the target weights; the optimized likelihood is still over those identities, not the total model probability assigned to an equivalent reconstructed vector.

   In a two-alias example where every possible generated sample decodes to exactly the same vector, changing model probabilities from `[.5,.5]` to `[.9999,.0001]` increases the equal-head objective by 1.956 nats despite identical decoded distributions. This is a distinction between token likelihood and vector likelihood, not an error in cross-entropy arithmetic.

3. Completed-site context retains representation identity.

   The recovered model's `embed_with_model_aux` returns physical pair contribution plus a learned adapter of the dictionary atom, coefficient-bin embedding, and contribution. The spatial transformer sums these embeddings. Exact sign aliases `(D,c)` and `(-D,-c)` therefore need not produce the same spatial context. Independent CPU probes verified identical physical vectors with different learned embeddings. This behavior is inherited from the successful FFHQ architecture.

   A CPU probe using actual checkpoint weights and 32 training plus 32 held-out Church rows measured projected adapter RMS 2.49 at step 9,780 and 7.67 at step 18,000, versus physical RMS 1.30 and 1.55. Those raw ratios must not be interpreted as identity information dominating: after centering across images and sites, adapter RMS is .543 and .850, versus physical RMS 1.283 and 1.532. The large common offset is material to interpreting this diagnostic. The synthetic alias test establishes non-invariance, not an actual Church alias rate or a causal FID effect.

4. The generalization failure survives metric and implementation checks.

   Validation uses 1,024 disjoint stage-2 holdout images and fixed stochastic targets. Its expected target distortion and target entropy remain identical across epochs. Atom CE rises from 8.571 at epoch 10 to 9.933 at epoch 30, while average logged training atom CE falls from 7.749 to 5.490. The coefficient change is much smaller. Only about .56% of sampled atom slots change; mean conditional atom-target entropy is about .021 nats. The stochastic teacher remains almost hard for supports. This is evidence of support generalization failure already with teacher-provided histories. It does not isolate the contribution of exposure bias during rollout.

   By epoch 36, held-out atom CE is 10.264, worse than uniform-atom prediction (9.704). A depth-specific training-frequency baseline scores 9.458 on the deterministic held-out cache; that latter comparison is descriptive because its labels are not the exact stochastic validation targets. A complete cache-count check found no deterministic held-out atom unseen in training at any depth, all normalized coefficients within the existing grid, and all class labels zero. This rules out those particular vocabulary, range, and conditioning mismatches. It does not imply that the neural model's sampled images are worse than an unconditional unigram image generator.

   The reported `joint_kl_per_pair` needs a separate caveat. Targets depend on a random candidate pool that the model does not observe. Averaging pool-conditioned KL includes irreducible disagreement between those targets and their mixture. Pool coefficient entropy near one nat cannot be read as the entropy of the overall stochastic teacher, and a high logged KL is not entirely reducible model error. Held-out CE deterioration under fixed targets remains a valid observation.

Independent numerical checks passed: all 16 positions of a tiny model using the actual production classes matched cached and parallel logits within 3.58e-7; perturbing future tokens or the current coefficient left the current predictions unchanged; changing the current atom affected only the coefficient prediction; earlier coefficients affected both heads. Eight equal microbatches produced the same averaged loss gradient as a global batch in a separate CPU reduction test. Physical scales are applied once. All trainable parameters receive gradients; only stage 1 is frozen. Initialization and optimizer state were fresh.

The production trainer/backbone/attention files match the recovered FFHQ sources. Important deliberate differences remain: Church uses 16,384 atoms and four depths instead of 2,048 and two; global batch 256 instead of 128; the new stochastic finite-pool teacher; equal atom/coefficient weights instead of 1.5:1; and no old .05 geometry loss. The old geometry loss multiplied incompatible marginal expectations, so restoring it is not an established correction. Same-precision tiny-model tests do not quantify the full trained model's FP32/TF32 training versus FP16 sampling differences.

The most useful next objective test is whether a revised proposal and loss give appropriate credit to known equivalent or nearby complete sparse reconstructions under the existing frozen coefficient grids, while preserving the registered distortion limit. This must include coordinated changes and proposal-coverage checks, not merely a higher temperature. A separate architecture ablation can feed completed spatial sites only their summed physical vector. Test these separately: earlier local native RQ also underperformed despite physical spatial inputs, so removing the adapter is not a demonstrated universal solution. Changing only the sampler cannot settle these objective/representation questions.

The [RQ-Transformer paper, equations 10–15](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.01941#S3.SS2) uses summed quantized vectors for spatial context and geometry-based soft code targets. It motivates checking these properties but does not validate a particular sparse-code replacement. No new paid training experiment was launched by this audit.
