LSUN Church stage 2: method shortlist, checked September 10, 2026

Follow-up: the proposed matched BAR-inspired pilot is now complete. It did not improve the initial unconditional-generation screen; see the [experiment results](lsun-church-bar-coefficients-2026-09-10.md). The recommendations below record the reasoning before that experiment.

**Recommendation: test a BAR-inspired masked-bit coefficient head first, with the existing Church tokenizer and autoregressive pair order.** FDPT-AR is the most relevant subsequent experiment for improving decoded samples without changing the coefficient representation. These are proposed adaptations; none of the reviewed papers establishes performance on our sparse Church representation.

Our fixed starting point is the ImageNet rFID 4.210914 tokenizer after its completed one-epoch Church fine-tune, with 16,384 atoms, four coefficients at each of 64 spatial sites, and 2,048 signed coefficient bins. The established epoch-50 AR checkpoint has a matched Church FID-50k reevaluation of 13.7246. Our small sign-only pilot improved complete-pattern accuracy but did not demonstrate reconstruction improvement. See the [run review](lsun-church-stage2-review-2026-09-10.md) and [pilot report](lsun-church-sign-pattern-pilot-2026-09-10.md).

The following published numbers concern class-conditional ImageNet-256, with each paper's tokenizer, training budget, and sampling settings. They are evidence of method capability, not comparable scores against our unconditional Church experiment or predictions of its attainable FID.

| Method | Evidence checked | Proposed adaptation | Relative implementation work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BAR, ICML 2026 | Masked generation of a token's bits conditioned on AR context; BAR-B reports guided gFID 1.13 at 415M parameters, BAR-L 0.99 at 1.1B. | Represent each coefficient bin ID with 11 bits; progressively generate these bits using the existing causal coefficient context. | Moderate; preserves tokenizer and pair order. |
| BitDance, February/March 2026 | Joint binary diffusion head; reported class-conditional ImageNet FID 1.24 at approximately 1B parameters. | Generate the same 11-bit coefficient representation with a small diffusion head. | Moderate; additional noise schedule and sampling loop. |
| MAR / Diffusion Loss, NeurIPS 2024 | A small conditional denoiser represents each continuous token's distribution; reported best ImageNet FID 1.55. | Model physical coefficients continuously, conditioned on atom identity and causal history. | Moderate; use raw coefficients and handle normalization and range. |
| FDPT-AR, August 2026 preprint | Post-training with decoded-image feature distributions and generated-context replay; TiTok-B-64 FID 2.42 to 1.43 under matched inference. | Fine-tune our established pair generator through the frozen sparse decoder, retaining both categorical token types. | Higher; sparse token readout needs a differentiable surrogate and decoded-image training. |

Sources: [BAR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09024v1), [BitDance project and results](https://bitdance.csuhan.com/), [MAR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11838v3), [FDPT-AR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00562v1).

BAR has [official training and sampling code](https://github.com/amazon-far/BAR). Its head is a small conditioned network, trained to fill masked bits and sampled through successive unmasking rounds. This is distinct from the one-shot independent-bit baseline. An especially relevant qualification is Table 1: without guidance, its 10-bit masked head scores 3.10 versus 2.80 for the categorical head, while at 12 bits it scores 2.10 versus 2.70. Our 11-bit coefficient case therefore needs a direct comparison. Also, BAR's learned FSQ representation and semantic encoder differ from our scalar coefficient IDs.

BitDance uses binary latents with learned structure, so its gains cannot simply be assumed to transfer to integer IDs. Keep atom IDs categorical initially: their numerical binary encoding need not reflect dictionary geometry. The [official repository](https://github.com/shallowdream204/BitDance) contains model code and checkpoints, but its README still describes the training instructions as forthcoming when checked. BAR is the more convenient first implementation reference.

The mature [MAR implementation](https://github.com/LTH14/mar) makes conditional diffusion a useful comparison. Our adaptation would learn a coefficient distribution, not regress to its average. A joint four-coefficient denoiser could model within-site dependencies, but requires all four supports to be available first. That changes the current interleaved atom/coefficient factorization and requires a matched support-first baseline; it is not a free replacement of the existing scalar head.

FDPT-AR directly addresses the discrepancy between token objectives and decoded-image quality. Its [official repository](https://github.com/CVL-UESTC/FDPT-AR) provides training recipes and checkpoints. Adaptation to our decoder must preserve the hard forward sum of coefficient-weighted atoms while supplying a surrogate gradient; the paper's ordinary codebook lookup is insufficient as-is. Feature-statistic training also needs monitoring for lost diversity and overfitting. Treat these August results as an early preprint, and confirm with held-out evaluation and the existing sampler.

Other relevant directions were checked:

- [Infinity, CVPR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04431v2): useful evidence for correction of generated-context errors. Its correction recomputes later residual targets after perturbing earlier codes; random bit flips alone were not the effective method. Our final OMP coefficients are not a multi-scale residual tokenizer, so a faithful adaptation needs residual recomputation. Do not simply add random sign flips and call that Infinity self-correction.
- [HART, ICLR 2025](https://hanlab.mit.edu/projects/hart) and [D2C, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17155v1): support hybrid discrete/continuous modeling. Their discrete codes carry a decodable coarse image, whereas our unsigned atom supports alone lack coefficient signs and strengths. Borrowing their architecture would require defining that conditioning carefully; HART's residual decoder is not a direct coefficient head.
- [SphereAR, ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24335): uses fixed-norm hyperspherical latents to stabilize generation. Directly normalizing our coefficient vectors would change reconstruction energy, so its full representation is incompatible with an unchanged tokenizer.
- [MTAR, August 26, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25386v1): auxiliary future-token prediction and representation regularization leave inference unchanged. It reports LlamaGen-L FID 3.80 to 2.85 at 300 epochs. This is a plausible later generalization experiment, but it does not specifically resolve atom/coefficient compatibility. The [linked code repository](https://github.com/yzh595/MTAR-Code) exists.
- [PixelCNN++](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.05517): its discretized mixture likelihood is an older, inexpensive control that assigns probabilities using ordered scalar values while retaining integer bins. It is not a current SOTA claim, but is worth comparing against a more elaborate 11-bit head.

The next controlled experiment should retain the same full Church training split, tokenizer hash, scales, 2,048-bin grid, causal pair order, atom predictor, and evaluation seeds. Fit the BAR-inspired coefficient head using pretrained AR context, then compare matched fine-tuning against an unchanged categorical continuation. A smaller ordered-mixture head is a useful capacity-efficient control. The experiment must start inference with every bit of the current coefficient unknown; revealed bits then come from its own predictions. Future coefficient targets must never enter the outer AR context.

First use held-out coefficient diagnostics and generated-history rollouts to identify obvious failures. Confirm candidates with unconditional generation, initially a small sample screen and then matched FID-50k and diversity checks. Masked-bit training cross-entropy is not the same quantity as the categorical head's exact token NLL, so those losses should not be compared numerically as if equivalent. Estimate the induced sampling distribution when likelihood comparison is required, or compare shared sample-based metrics.

Moving coefficient signs into doubled atom IDs remains a separate exact representation ablation. It preserves the nominal bit count: 14 atom bits + 11 coefficient bits becomes 15 signed-atom bits + 10 magnitude bits. Integer relabeling by itself does not improve the modeled distribution. Keep this change separate from the prediction-head experiment so that any improvement has an identifiable cause.

This review added documentation only; it did not launch training or alter the existing models.
