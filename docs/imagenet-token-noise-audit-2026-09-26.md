The current ImageNet coefficient noise is correctly expressed in physical units and is small in both tokenizer latents and the learned transformer inputs. The generated-token screen instead shows a mismatch in covariance and spatial variation. The cause of the FID gap is not isolated by this audit; fixed image views, deterministic atom targets, and an uncalibrated ImageNet sampler are the strongest concrete recipe differences to test next. Reconstruction rFID 4.21 measures the tokenizer's reconstruction distribution and does not establish that its token distribution is easier for an autoregressive prior to learn.

The frozen diagnostic checkpoint is the epoch-50 best, update 31,750, FID50k 33.2917767. Its full model state loaded strictly. All diagnostics ran on CPU while four-H200 training continued at approximately 1,700 images/sec. Production configuration and weights were not changed. Exact target moments use 16,384 independently selected training images, one random spatial site per image. The decoder probe uses 32 images. The prior prediction probe uses 64 training images, and the generation screen uses 512 generated images and two independent, class-matched sets of 512 clean training examples. These are diagnostics, not additional FID50k evaluations or held-out model selection.

Relative to coefficient RMS, the current SD is **1.71%, 2.92%, 4.51%, and 7.05%** across depths. The scale vector `[8.2033653, 4.2656388, 3.0662174, 1.8273425]` agrees between checkpoint and token-cache metadata and is used by both embeddings and decoding. The stochastic input draw was checked against the exact same distribution used by the soft target. A legacy scalar `coeff_scale` differs between metadata and launch options but is superseded by the identical explicit per-depth scale vector; it is not a decoding scale mismatch.

In a paired FP32 decoder check, PSNR relative to the clean continuous-coefficient reconstruction is **42.30 dB** with current noise, **36.25 dB** with previous noise, and **71.70 dB** with hard nearest-bin quantization. These compare decoder perturbations; they are not reconstruction FID against original images. Directly transplanting a numerical temperature of 0.5 from RQ to normalized LASER coefficients would produce a very different and much larger perturbation.

Changing a coefficient ID does not necessarily mean a large perturbation. Current noise changes the nearest-bin token at 92.3–98.3% of sampled positions because bins are fine. To check whether the learned coefficient embedding amplified this, 64 independent target draws were measured at each of 512 sites. Expected squared perturbation relative to clean signal energy is **0.0220%** after the spatial input projection, and **0.0251%, 0.0469%, 0.0753%, 0.1369%** in the projected cumulative depth representations. Individual learned pair representations have ratios **0.0298%, 0.1099%, 0.2903%, 1.0410%**. These measurements do not show a large hidden perturbation at the transformer input.

The frozen prior predicts broader coefficient distributions than a per-image teacher, but that comparison alone does not diagnose erroneous noise. On the 64-image training probe, predicted full-distribution SD is `[1.684, 1.137, 0.865, 0.680]`, while RMS error of its conditional means is `[1.513, 1.105, 0.832, 0.662]`. Ratios of observed target error to predicted variance are approximately `[0.81, 0.96, 0.94, 0.98]`; much of that spread is commensurate with prediction uncertainty in this probe. This is not held-out calibration evidence. With correct atom histories and the current coefficient nucleus filter, conditional coefficient reconstruction error is about 9.04% of clean latent energy versus 0.0741% from the teacher itself. That difference includes legitimate multimodality and prediction error; it must not be interpreted as 9% artificial teacher noise. Lowering sampling temperature reduces paired reconstruction error but has not been shown here to improve generation FID.

One seed and 512 images cannot establish a final distributional result or the cause of the FID gap. CPU BF16 generation is also not bitwise equivalent to the production GPU sampler.

The differences from the released RQ recipe are concrete:

- **Atom targets:** current LASER atoms come from a fixed deterministic OMP cache and have one-hot targets. The [released RQ configuration](https://github.com/kakaobrain/rq-vae-transformer/blob/main/configs/imagenet256/stage2/in256-rqtransformer-8x8x4-1400M.yaml) specifies stochastic codes and soft cross-entropy; its [quantizer](https://github.com/kakaobrain/rq-vae-transformer/blob/main/rqvae/models/rqvae/quantizations.py) samples distance-weighted codes and updates the residual before choosing later codes. Perturbing only LASER coefficients does not reproduce that mechanism. A LASER stochastic-support experiment needs a consistent joint teacher and, where applicable, recomputed coefficient normalizers and prefix-conditioned atom posteriors.
- **Image views:** the current cache records `resize256_center_crop256`, one view per image. The [released ImageNet transforms](https://github.com/kakaobrain/rq-vae-transformer/blob/main/rqvae/img_datasets/transforms.py) use fresh random crops and horizontal flips for training. More epochs over the same cache do not restore those views.
- **Sampling:** current atom top-k 2,048 was transferred from an FFHQ vocabulary fraction, not selected by an ImageNet FID sweep. On the teacher-conditioned probe it retains **97.3%, 87.6%, 81.3%, 75.1%** of atom probability by depth. The fixed cutoff affects deeper events more strongly. This is a hypothesis for a matched sampler sweep, not proof that any alternate cutoff wins. Repeated-atom probability removed at sampling is negligible on this probe, so that particular training/sampling mask difference is not a strong suspect.

The [official released models](https://github.com/kakaobrain/rq-vae-transformer#pretrained-checkpoints) list 1.4B ImageNet FIDs 11.56 and 8.71 with different tokenizer checkpoints, without rejection sampling; the parenthesized results use rejection sampling. This audit did not rerun those baselines. Neither a superior reconstruction score nor comparable parameter count guarantees a superior generation score.

The next experiments should isolate the differences: first a frozen-checkpoint sampler comparison with matched generation seeds and confirmation by FID50k; then training controls for fresh image views and stochastic atom targets. Further reduction of the already-small coefficient teacher temperature is a lower-priority ablation, not an evidence-backed fix for the entire gap. No configuration was selected using the 512-image latent screen, and no new production launch was made.
